METHOD
  ADV20 is recomputed exactly as capacity.py computes it for RUN.txt:
      capacity.py:54  nse = (d["exchange"] == "NSE") if "exchange" in d.columns else False
      capacity.py:55  tv = (d["close"] * d["volume"]).where(nse)
      capacity.py:56  cache[key] = (d["close"], tv.rolling(20, min_periods=1).mean().shift(1))
  i.e. the mean close x volume over the NSE rows among the 20 file rows before the fill
  date. The recomputed maxima equal the RUN.txt "largest" figures for all four arms
  (53,824.47 / 38,557.66 / 67,697.63 / 57,815.38).
  Fill value = qty x fill_price from COSTS_<tag>.csv. Slippage is the slippage_rs column
  (flat SLIPPAGE = 0.0015, results/engine_core.py:593 and :608); impact is 0 because no
  --impact was given. Position P&L = the gain of every COST_LOTS lot with this symbol and
  buy_date, plus any shares still held on the last day marked at that day's price, gross
  of charges. Membership is results/survivorship.py Membership.is_member on the fill date,
  loaded as run.py:1553 loads it.
  Liquidity filter: none applies. The research profile has no participation cap
  (profiles.py:88  "research":  {"participation_cap": None}), and neither
  results/build_scores.py nor results/engine_v2_final.py reads volume to select or size.
  Every fill below is unconstrained by liquidity in both pit and static.

CONCLUSION
  The 38,558% to 67,698% maxima are a measurement artefact in capacity.py, not a pit rule
  and not a fill the engine priced oddly. All four come from one fill, ALKYLAMINE on
  2012-01-13. Its file has 19 BSE rows and 1 NSE row in the 20 sessions before that date,
  and the NSE row is a gap-filled row from a second source (source kite, _gap_filled 1)
  with volume 10. capacity.py drops the BSE rows and, with min_periods=1, takes that one
  row's Rs 169 as the 20-day average. Over all 20 rows the same fill is 46% to 81% of
  average traded value.
  The same mechanism produces the next tier: OLECTRA 2012 to 2014 (1 to 3 NSE rows, all
  gap-filled; 2,353% to 4,095% on NSE rows, 428% to 745% on all rows). 26 of the 73 W2 pit
  buys above 100% rest on fewer than 5 NSE rows, against 9 of 71 in W2 static and 0 of 12
  in W1 pit.
  Why pit shows it and static does not: pit admits names that were index members in
  2009 to 2014 and are not in today's list. ALKYLAMINE, RTNINDIA and SOMANYCERA have no
  file in the static folder at all. Their early years in the with-survivorship files are
  mostly BSE rows. Static holds today's members, whose early files are mostly NSE. W1
  starts in 2019, after the files turn NSE, so W1 pit has no such fill.
  Separately from the artefact, some fills are genuinely too large for the name at Rs 1m
  start capital compounding to tens of millions: RTNINDIA 2020-04-17 (948,004 shares at
  Rs 1.70, 15 NSE rows, 5 zero-volume padded rows, 2,279% on NSE rows, 3,039% on all
  rows) and ABREL 2021 to 2024 (20 NSE rows, 311% to 709%). ABREL also leads W2 static
  (up to 1,346%) and W1 pit. The count above 100% is about the same in W2 pit (73) and
  W2 static (71); the capacity problem is the absence of a liquidity rule in the research
  profile, in both modes, not something pit computes differently.
  Effect on results: in W2 pit, buys above 100% of ADV20 carry 8.6% (v1), 0.7% (v2),
  14.5% (v3) and 9.1% (v4) of position P&L; in v1, v3 and v4 most of it is the one
  2024 buy, ABREL on 2024-02-23 (the 2024 row of each arm in table (a)). The
  ALKYLAMINE fill itself lost Rs 9,074 in v1.

FIX OPTIONS (none applied)
  1. capacity.py: require a minimum number of NSE rows (for example 10 of 20) and report
     the fill as not measurable otherwise, and exclude _gap_filled rows from the average.
     Changes only the RUN.txt capacity lines; no result moves.
  2. capacity.py: when NSE rows are sparse, fall back to all rows (NSE + BSE) and say so
     in the line. Understates true market volume on mixed days, but avoids Rs 169 averages.
  3. Strategy side (changes results, needs a decision): run the comparison under the
     tradeable profile, whose participation cap already limits a buy to a multiple of the
     prior-20-session median volume, or add a minimum-liquidity screen on candidates.
     Either changes every published figure and is out of scope here.
