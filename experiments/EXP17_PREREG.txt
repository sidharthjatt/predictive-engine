EXP17 -- A SECOND REGIME INPUT ALONGSIDE BREADTH
================================================================================
Written BEFORE the experiment is run. Nothing below may be changed once a result
has been seen. If a rule here turns out to be badly specified, the experiment is
abandoned and re-registered, not edited.

Trial count: this is the 18th tested idea on this dataset. 17 tested so far,
1 accepted (top-8 selection). Two exposure experiments were rejected immediately
before this one: EXP15 (panic exit) and EXP16 (index trend gate).


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE QUESTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The system sets exposure = breadth, where breadth is the fraction of the universe
with positive 20-day momentum. Measured behaviour of that rule:

    breadth < 0.35 (18 of 93 rebalances) -> next 20 days average +0.36%, up 44%
    breadth > 0.65 (31 of 93 rebalances) -> next 20 days average +2.76%, up 81%
    Spearman IC of breadth vs next 20-day return: +0.19

So breadth carries real information. The complaint is not about the signal, it is
about deployment: average capital deployed is 55%, and breadth exceeds 0.80 on
only 12 of 93 rebalances. In a rising market the system is not fully invested.

EXP15 and EXP16 both tried to fix this by re-shaping the SAME signal, and both
failed. This experiment asks a different question: does a second, largely
independent regime signal add anything on top of breadth?


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE CANDIDATE, AND HOW IT WAS CHOSEN  (declared, because it matters)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Six candidate signals were screened on this same data, none of them derived from
20-day momentum:

    signal            corr w/ breadth    IC vs fwd20    |t|
    dispersion_60          +0.12            +0.221      2.16
    skew_60                -0.12            -0.217      2.13
    idx_vol_60             +0.05            +0.132      1.27
    downside_ratio         -0.43            -0.143      1.38
    breadth_ma200          +0.46            -0.022      0.21
    vol_of_vol             +0.11            +0.006      0.06

dispersion_60 is taken forward. It has the highest IC and is nearly uncorrelated
with breadth, which is the property being sought.

THE HONEST PROBLEM WITH THAT: it was selected as the best of six on the same 93
observations it will now be tested on. A |t| of 2.16 is not impressive after
six looks -- a Bonferroni-style correction would want roughly 2.9. The screen is
therefore treated as hypothesis generation, not evidence, and Gate D below exists
specifically to catch the case where this is noise.

    dispersion_60 = 60-day mean of the cross-sectional standard deviation of
                    daily returns across the universe.

Economic reading: high dispersion means stocks are moving apart, which is the
condition under which a cross-sectional ranking system has something to rank.
Low dispersion means everything moves together and selection cannot add value.
This is a different claim from breadth, which measures direction, not spread.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. THE FUNCTIONAL FORM  (fixed here, before any result)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    d_pct   = percentile rank of dispersion_60 within its own trailing 3 years,
              computed causally (no future data), in [0, 1]
    tilt    = 0.5 + d_pct                      -> range [0.5, 1.5]
    exposure = clip(breadth * tilt, 0.0, 1.0)

Notes on why this form and not another:

  - Multiplicative, so when breadth is zero exposure is zero. The second signal
    may not override the first; it may only scale it.
  - Percentile rather than a raw level, so no absolute threshold is fitted and the
    signal is scale-free across regimes.
  - The [0.5, 1.5] band is symmetric around 1.0, so the rule cannot raise average
    deployment by construction -- it must earn any gain by timing.
  - Trailing 3 years for the percentile window. Not tuned. Chosen because it is
    the same order as the 252-day windows already in the system.

NO variants of this form will be tried. If it fails, the failure is recorded and
the question is closed, exactly as EXP15 and EXP16 were closed.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. ACCEPT / REJECT RULE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ACCEPT only if ALL FOUR hold:

  A. Sharpe is not lower in EITHER universe (58 and 74).

  B. CAGR improves by more than 1.0 pt in BOTH universes.
     Rationale: the whole point is deployment, so a return improvement is the
     claim being made. A Sharpe-only gain does not pass -- EXP15 and EXP16 both
     produced small Sharpe wobbles in one universe while failing the other.

  C. Both sub-periods pass in both universes:
     58: 2019-2022 and 2023-2026    74: 2019-2022 and 2023-2025
     "Pass" means Sharpe not lower and CAGR not lower in that sub-period.

  D. NOISE CONTROL. Re-run with the dispersion series randomly shuffled across
     rebalance dates, 20 independent shuffles, same seed set recorded. The real
     signal's CAGR improvement must exceed the 90th percentile of the shuffled
     improvements in BOTH universes.
     If shuffled dispersion produces a similar gain, the gain is not information.
     This gate exists because the candidate was chosen from a screen of six.

REJECT on any single failure. One batch. No re-runs with a different tilt band,
a different percentile window, or skew_60 substituted in.

skew_60 is NOT tested here and is NOT a fallback. If EXP17 is rejected, testing
the runner-up from the same screen is simply continuing to mine the same data.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. HONEST EXPECTATION, RECORDED IN ADVANCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Rejection, most likely at Gate D.

Reasons: the Fundamental Law states IR is approximately IC x sqrt(BR). Measured
here, IC is about 0.036 and breadth about 27, giving IR near 0.97 -- which is
close to what the system achieves. An exposure rule changes neither IC nor BR,
which is why twelve portfolio-construction experiments and then EXP15 and EXP16
all failed. There is no strong reason this one is different in kind.

The reason to run it anyway: EXP15 and EXP16 both re-shaped the existing signal.
This is the first attempt to add an independent one, and that is a genuinely
different question. It deserves one clean test.
