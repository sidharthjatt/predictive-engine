### TWO MECHANICS THAT ARE OFTEN MISUNDERSTOOD -- know these

1. FULL COMPOUNDING. At each rebalance:
       port_val   = current market value of holdings + all cash
       invest_val = port_val * exposure * 0.98
   Profits are never set aside. If the portfolio grows to Rs 12L, breadth is
   applied to Rs 12L. This is why Rs 10L became Rs 31L.

2. EXISTING HOLDINGS ARE NEVER RESIZED. The engine skips any symbol already held:
       if s == "_exposure" or s in shares: continue
   Only NEW positions are sized against the target exposure. A held stock is sold
   only when its rank falls below 16.

   Consequence: actual invested % can drift far above the target. A real example
   from the log -- breadth 3.33%, so target exposure 3.33%, but actual invested
   79.86%, a gap of 76 points. The new buys were small and correct; the 11 existing
   holdings simply were not trimmed.

   THIS IS THE ANSWER to "why does it still buy in a falling market". The buys are
   tiny. The problem is that old positions are not cut. Section [D] of the daily log
   now prints this gap on every rebalance line.

### AN OPEN DESIGN QUESTION (not tuning -- worth testing)
Enforce the exposure target on the WHOLE portfolio, not just on new buys. At each
rebalance, if actual invested % exceeds the target, trim existing holdings back to
target proportionally.

This is not a new threshold or a tuned knob -- it applies the target the system
already computes. It is the only change that would genuinely raise cash in a
falling market, because it addresses the real cause (holdings are never trimmed)
rather than the exposure formula, which has already been tested six times.

Honest expected cost: higher turnover, higher TC, and trimming in bull markets too
(breadth 70% while holdings sit at 90%), which will cut upside. Sharpe may fall.
Pre-register the rule before running it: Sharpe must not fall in either universe,
and both sub-periods must pass. If it fails, record it in
rejected_experiments_REPORT.txt and close the exposure question for good.

label is trained on a 20-day forward horizon, so acting at 10 days uses the signal
at half its horizon, and turnover (and TC) roughly doubles. It would also not fix
the falling-market complaint, because holdings still would not be trimmed.

## 7. LESSONS WORTH KEEPING
================================================================================

 1. When a backtest looks too good, look for the bug first. Long-short showed
    2526% CAGR; it was a double-counting bug. Litmus test first, performance second.
 2. N_eff = (1+lambda)/(1-lambda); CoM = lambda/(1-lambda). Useful for sanity
    checking any EWMA scheme before trusting its result.
 3. Never change two things at once. The EWMA episode changed weighting and horizon
    together and nothing could be attributed.
 4. Always test on BOTH universes. Results frequently disagree, and one universe
    alone would have produced the wrong conclusion.
 5. Sub-period splits are more trustworthy than full-period results. breadth-delta
    k=2 won the full period on the 74 but lost both halves -- it was lucky
    compounding.
 6. Determinism matters. Six byte-identical runs are what make "top-8 raised Sharpe"
    a claim rather than a guess.
 7. Healthy suspicion pays. When six runs came back identical Sid asked whether
    something was wrong; a seed-rotation test confirmed genuine computation.
 8. No moving averages, no indicators. This is Sid's standing instruction and is
    why the Faber 200-day trend gate was dropped.
 9. Reconciliation checks earn their keep. The daily log's cash identity caught a
    real configuration mismatch the moment it appeared.
10. An output that hardcodes its own conclusion will eventually lie. Every printed
    verdict is now computed from the current run.
11. PRESENTATION RULE (permanent): always write "Name -- descriptive tagline with
    keywords", never a bare project name, everywhere.

"The system builds 17 features from daily price and volume, normalises them
cross-sectionally, and ranks stocks every month with a LightGBM 10-seed ensemble
using a 32-day purge and an expanding walk-forward window. It buys the top 8,
sets total exposure from market breadth, sizes positions by inverse volatility,
and executes on a close-signal / next-open-fill basis with real Zerodha costs.

The result is a drawdown of about 17% against the index's 36.5%, roughly half,
in both universes. Risk-adjusted return is comparable rather than better: Sharpe
1.36 against 1.29 on the 58, and 1.27 against 1.29 on the 74. Raw return is behind,
because the strategy holds around 35% cash and I model that cash as earning
nothing.

I tested leakage four ways -- purging alone caught 5.4 points of fake CAGR, and a
shuffle test confirmed the engine itself is clean. Inverse-vol and breadth both
validated across seeds and sub-periods. Twelve improvement ideas were tested and
rejected, with the accept/reject rule written down first; only top-8 concentration
survived. The whole system reproduces from scratch in 25 minutes with one command,
and every trading day is reconciled -- cash, equity and share counts all tie out
on 1,842 of 1,842 days.

Caveats: no capital gains tax, survivorship bias in the universe, and the edge is
not statistically significant. To make this a product it needs a less efficient
universe or new data, not more tuning."
