EXP20 -- TRADEABILITY FILTER
================================================================================
Written BEFORE the experiment is run. Nothing below may be changed once a result
has been seen. If a rule turns out to be badly specified, the experiment is
abandoned and re-registered, not edited.

Trial count: the 21st tested idea on this dataset. 20 tested so far, 1 accepted
(top-8 selection). EXP18 was the 20th and was rejected; EXP19 was never run.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE MEASURED PROBLEM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The liquidity measurement (diagnostics/liquidity_participation.txt) found that the
MidCap150 strategy holds positions that cannot be entered at the price the
backtest assumes, at the backtest's OWN size of Rs 10,00,000:

  - maximum participation 1,614% of prior-20-day median daily volume, on AIIL
  - four AIIL fills above 100% of prior-20-day MDV
  - p90 of 282% in the under-Rs-10 price band
  - 22 of 985 fills above 10% participation

Modelling depth natively rather than as flat slippage (diagnostics/
depth_compare.txt) costs mid 1.80 CAGR points and 0.10 Sharpe: 29.16% -> 27.36%,
Sharpe 2.00 -> 1.90, with 19 orders walking the book. n100 loses 0.01 points.

An order for sixteen days of a stock's median volume does not execute at the
opening price. It moves the price, or it does not execute.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE CLAIM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Excluding names that cannot absorb a full position is a TRADEABILITY CONSTRAINT of
the same category as transaction costs, not a parameter choice. Costs are already
modelled; capacity is not. A backtest that reports returns from positions it could
not have entered is reporting something other than a strategy.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE RULE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
At each rebalance, a name is INELIGIBLE if a full target position would exceed 10%
of its prior-20-day median daily volume, at Rs 10,00,000 starting capital.

  full target position value = START_CAPITAL / TOP_N = 10,00,000 / 8 = Rs 1,25,000
  shares required            = 1,25,000 / price on the decision date
  ineligible if                shares required > 0.10 x median(volume, prior 20 days)

Ineligible names are removed from the candidate set before the top-8 is taken.
Everything else -- the model, the 17 features, the 10 seeds, the 32-day purge, the
monthly expanding walk-forward, TOP_N=8, BUFFER=16, breadth, inverse-vol sizing,
SAFETY=0.98, CASH_YIELD=0.0 -- is unchanged.

CAUSALITY
    The median volume uses the 20 trading days STRICTLY BEFORE the decision date,
    and the price is the decision date's own close, both knowable when the decision
    is made. The filter is recomputed at every rebalance.

    Report the per-year eligible count as causality evidence. AN IDENTICAL COUNT IN
    EVERY YEAR MEANS THE FILTER IS NOT CAUSAL AND THE RUN IS VOID -- report that
    rather than any performance number.

THE THRESHOLD IS FIXED HERE AND IS NOT TUNED
    10% of median daily volume, measured against a position sized at STARTING
    capital. 10% because it is the level the liquidity measurement already used to
    flag fills, so the filter and the diagnostic speak the same language. Starting
    capital because it makes the threshold a fixed rupee amount that does not drift
    with the equity curve, which keeps the filter causal and independent of the
    result it is being judged on.

    No other value will be tried. No price-based cut-off will be substituted.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ACCEPT ONLY IF ALL FIVE HOLD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  A. mid's CAGR under DEPTH_MODE="volume" IMPROVES.
     The filter must earn its place under realistic depth, not under the
     unlimited-depth assumption it exists to escape. This is the point of the
     experiment: if it only looks good when depth is assumed infinite, it has
     not addressed the problem it was built for.

  B. mid's Sharpe is not lower under "volume" mode.

  C. Both sub-periods pass, 2019-2022 and 2023 onward: Sharpe not lower AND CAGR
     not lower in each.

  D. Maximum fill participation falls below 50% of prior-20-day MDV, and NO fill
     exceeds 100%.

  E. n100 is unchanged to within 0.10 CAGR. It has only 3 fills above 10%
     participation, so a filter that moves it materially is doing something other
     than removing untradeable names, and the something else would be unmeasured.

REJECT on any single failure. One batch. No variants -- no 5% or 20% cap, no
price-based cut-off substituted in.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IS REPORTED, WHATEVER THE OUTCOME
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
For BOTH universes and BOTH depth modes ("unlimited" and "volume"):
CAGR, Sharpe, MaxDD, trade count, and fill participation distribution.

Against BOTH benchmarks for each universe:
  - the published cap-weighted index (NIFTY100.csv, NIFTYMIDCAP150.csv)
  - the own-universe equal-weight buy&hold, RECOMPUTED ON THE FILTERED UNIVERSE,
    because dropping names changes the buy&hold too. Comparing a filtered strategy
    against an unfiltered buy&hold would credit the filter with a difference that
    is partly just a different basket.

Plus the per-year eligible count (the causality evidence) and the per-year count of
names excluded.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HONEST EXPECTATION, RECORDED IN ADVANCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
mid's CAGR under UNLIMITED depth will fall, because AIIL and the cheap names carry
real return -- the leave-one-out work already showed how concentrated mid's edge is
in a few names. That fall is expected and is not by itself a failure.

The question the experiment actually asks is whether it falls by LESS than the
1.80 points that realistic depth already costs. If the filter gives up 1.0 point of
unlimited-depth return to avoid 1.80 points of depth cost, it is worth having. If
it gives up more than 1.80, it is not: the honest thing would be to keep the names
and pay the impact.

Genuinely uncertain. The filter removes names by capacity, not by expected return,
so there is no reason it should preferentially remove winners -- but mid's edge is
concentrated in exactly the cheap, thin names the filter targets, and that is the
one structural reason to expect it to fail.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
OUTPUT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Separate script. Nothing written into results/, results_mid/ or results_n100/.
The results go to diagnostics/. English only. No tuning.
