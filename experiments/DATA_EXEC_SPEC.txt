================================================================================
CHECK A -- WHICH COLUMN CARRIES THE BAD VALUE, close OR adj_close?
================================================================================
WHAT IS ALREADY ESTABLISHED
    diagnostics/leakage_check4_corpactions.txt measured that close and adj_close
    disagree on 702 of 173,612 rows on n100 (0.404%) and 1,205 of 246,088 on mid
    (0.490%), and that 89% and 92% of those disagreements are ISOLATED SINGLE
    DAYS with a longest run of 5. A dividend or split adjustment restates every
    row before the ex-date, producing one long contiguous block. A median block
    length of ONE is the opposite signature: sporadic bad values.

    WHAT WAS NOT ESTABLISHED IS WHICH COLUMN IS WRONG. That matters and it is not
    symmetric: engine_core.build_panel:180 selects close and ignores adj_close,
    so A BAD VALUE IN close IS IN THE FEATURES AND IN THE RETURNS, while a bad
    value in adj_close is inert.

THE DECISIVE TEST -- OHLC INTERNAL CONSISTENCY, NO EXTERNAL TRUTH REQUIRED
    The raw CSVs carry open, high, low, close AND adj_close on every row. A
    closing price must lie within that session's own range:

        low <= close <= high

    IF close[d] FALLS OUTSIDE [low[d], high[d]], close IS BAD, PROVABLY, from the
    row itself. The same test is applied to adj_close on the same rows. This
    needs no corporate-action data and no external source, which is precisely why
    it is the primary test -- Check 4 was limited by having neither.

    The bounds are taken from the SAME row, so an adjustment applied to close but
    not to high/low would also show here. That ambiguity is reported if it
    arises rather than assumed away.

THE CORROBORATING TEST -- SPIKE AND REVERT
    A single bad price produces a large move on day d and a near-equal opposite
    move on d+1, because the series returns to its true level. A real price move
    does not revert by construction.

    For every disagreement day, on each column independently:
        r(d), r(d+1), and the reversal ratio  -r(d+1) / r(d)
    A ratio near +1 with a large |r(d)| is the signature of a bad value on d.

    THIS IS CORROBORATING, NOT DECISIVE. A genuine one-day crash followed by a
    genuine rebound also reverts. It is reported beside the bounds test and is
    never used alone to condemn a value.

REPORTED
    Per universe: the count of disagreement rows whose close violates its own
    OHLC bounds, and the count whose adj_close does; the symbols and dates;
    the magnitude of each violation in percent of price; and the same rows'
    reversal ratios. Every violating row is listed if there are fewer than 40,
    otherwise the worst 40 by magnitude with the full set written to CSV.

    AND, BECAUSE IT IS THE CONSEQUENCE THAT MATTERS: for any bad value found in
    close, whether that row lies inside the backtest window, and whether the
    EXTREME_RET guard (EXTREME_RET_LO = -0.35, HI = 0.55) would have masked the
    return it produces.

WRITTEN PREDICTION
    1. MOST DISAGREEMENT ROWS WILL VIOLATE NO BOUNDS IN EITHER COLUMN. A
       dividend-adjusted adj_close sits legitimately outside that day's raw
       high/low, so if adj_close is a genuine adjusted series I expect MANY
       adj_close bounds violations and FEW close violations.
    2. I EXPECT THE BAD VALUES TO BE IN adj_close, NOT close. Check 4 already
       found adj_close moving the wrong way on well-known event dates -- ADANIENT
       2023-02-03 close -26.1% against adj_close +1.2%, YESBANK 2020-03-09 close
       -21.0% against adj_close +31.6%. On those dates the close series matches
       what the market did.
    3. IF ANY close VALUE VIOLATES ITS OWN BOUNDS, THAT IS A BAD VALUE IN THE
       TRADED COLUMN and it is reported first, before anything else in Check A.
       I expect this count to be small. I do not expect it to be zero.

CHECK B -- EXECUTION TIMING, FROM THE FILL RECORD
================================================================================
THE CLAIM UNDER TEST
    The design is signal at the CLOSE of day t, fill at the OPEN of day t+1.
    HANDOFF_SUMMARY states it; engine_core's leakage checklist asserted it as a
    hardcoded PASS until 2026-08-29, when that label was changed to NOT CHECKED
    BY THIS RUN. NOTHING HAS EVER VERIFIED IT AGAINST THE FILL RECORD.

THE EVIDENCE
    nautilus/reports/{n100,mid}/fills.csv, written by the Nautilus port. 977 and
    973 fills respectively. Each row carries instrument_id, order_side, last_qty,
    last_px and ts_event.

    THE FILL PRICE IS THE INSTRUMENT. If a fill executed at the next session's
    open, last_px must reconstruct from that session's open. If it executed at
    the signal day's close, or at the fill day's close, it will reconstruct from
    those instead. The three are different numbers on almost every row, so the
    test discriminates.

RECONSTRUCTION, STATED BEFORE RUNNING
    The port applies slippage against the trade and rounds to a 0.05 tick grid
    (nt_verify.py:120 and :163 both pass tick_round=True). So for a fill on
    date d, symbol s:

        expected_BUY  = tick(open[s, d] * (1 + SLIPPAGE))
        expected_SELL = tick(open[s, d] * (1 - SLIPPAGE))

    with SLIPPAGE = 0.0015 and tick() rounding to the nearest 0.05.

    EACH FILL IS TESTED AGAINST FOUR CANDIDATES, not one:
        (a) that day's OPEN      -- the design
        (b) that day's CLOSE     -- would mean same-day close execution
        (c) the PREVIOUS session's CLOSE -- would mean signal-day close execution
        (d) the PREVIOUS session's OPEN
    and the report says which candidate each fill matches. A test that only
    checked (a) could not distinguish "matches the open" from "matches everything
    because the tolerance is loose".

    TOLERANCE: half a tick, 0.025, because the grid itself is 0.05. Stated in
    advance and not widened afterwards.

    ADDENDUM 2026-09-16 -- WHAT THE DEPARTURE FROM THIS PARAGRAPH RESTS ON.
    THE SPEC ABOVE IS NOT CHANGED. This records the basis of the implementation
    that no longer follows it, so the next person to touch tick() knows what they
    are weighing.

    results/check_b_exec_timing.py does NOT tick-round. Its tick() rounds to 2
    decimals and its tolerance is 0.005, not the half-tick 0.025 above. The
    docstring gives the reason: "only 210 of 977 fill prices lie on a 0.05 grid,
    and the un-rounded open*(1+/-slip) matches every fill to within EXACTLY 0.005
    -- the signature of 2-decimal storage, not tick rounding."

    THAT MEASUREMENT WAS TAKEN ON AN ORPHANED ARTEFACT. The 977 fills came from
    nautilus/reports/<universe>/fills.csv, a path the port STOPPED WRITING at
    commit 8568b78 on 2026-09-05 02:33, which added an arm segment. The file read
    on 2026-09-09 was a leftover that nothing was maintaining, and under that
    layout one shared fills.csv per universe held whichever arm ran LAST,
    unlabelled -- so the 977 fills cannot be attributed to a strategy either.

    RE-MEASURED 2026-09-16, on a NAMED arm at the current path
    (nautilus/reports/n100/v2/fills.csv, segment from nt_run.reports_segment):

        925 of 965 fill prices lie on the 0.05 grid   (95.9%)

    which is what THIS PARAGRAPH says the port does. On the 2-decimal
    reconstruction only 224 of 965 fills match the day's open, uniformly across
    all four arms and both universes (23.2% to 24.9%); eight sampled mismatches
    all reconstruct exactly as the TICK-ROUNDED open.

    NOTHING WAS CHANGED ON THE STRENGTH OF THAT. Which rounding the port applies
    is a fact about the port and belongs to this spec, not to one session's
    measurement -- which is precisely the mistake being recorded here. The figure
    is printed unconditionally by the probe ("on 0.05 grid ... of ...") so the
    contradiction cannot be true and unnoticed while it is unresolved.

ALSO CHECKED
    1. Every fill date is a trading day on the panel's own calendar.
    2. Every fill date is the session immediately AFTER a rebalance decision date,
       read from daily_decisions_*.csv -- so the fill is not merely at an open,
       but at the RIGHT open.
    3. No fill is dated before the first decision or after the window ends.

REPORTED
    Count checked, count matching each of the four candidates, and EVERY
    exception with its date, symbol, side, fill price and the four candidate
    prices. If the count of exceptions is zero, that is stated as a count and not
    as a verdict word.

WRITTEN PREDICTION
    1. Essentially all fills match candidate (a), the fill day's open. Confidence
       high -- the Nautilus port already verifies against the research engine at
       92 of 92 rebalances, which would be hard to achieve on the wrong price.
    2. A SMALL NUMBER OF ROWS MAY BE AMBIGUOUS, matching more than one candidate,
       where open and close are within half a tick of each other on a quiet day
       in a low-priced name. That is a limitation of the instrument, not an
       exception, and is reported as a separate count.
    3. I expect zero fills matching (b) or (c) alone. If any exist, THAT IS AN
       EXECUTION-TIMING DEFECT and it is reported first.

WHAT THESE TWO CHECKS CANNOT DO
================================================================================
 1. Check A cannot say which value is CORRECT, only which is internally
    inconsistent. A row where both columns satisfy their bounds but disagree is
    unresolved by this method and is counted as such.
 2. Check A has no corporate-action source. Unchanged from Check 4.
 3. Check B tests the PORT's fills. The port is verified against the research
    engine elsewhere, but this check does not re-verify that link, so it speaks
    directly to the port and only indirectly to the research engine.
 4. Neither check addresses leakage, survivorship or the trial denominator.

English only. No verdict string is hardcoded. NOTHING IS CORRECTED.
